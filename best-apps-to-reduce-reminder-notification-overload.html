Best Apps to Reduce Reminder Notification Overload

An honest roundup of the ways to quiet a noisy reminder app on iPhone, from the free built-in settings to a daily-digest layer to full task managers, with a fair note on where each one fits.

Updated July 2026
The short answer

If your reminders live in Apple Reminders and you just want the alert stream to calm down, the honest shortlist is small. Try the free built-in settings first: the Today Notification and Scheduled Summary get you part of the way at no cost. If you want a real single daily digest while keeping Apple Reminders, Remindfully is the featured pick here, a one-time $4.99 layer that turns the stream into one calm digest with urgent items still breaking through. If you are willing to leave Apple Reminders entirely, a full task manager like Todoist or Things 3 gives you fine-grained control over notifications, at the cost of moving your whole system.

"Reminder notification overload" usually is not caused by a bad app. It is caused by Apple Reminders doing exactly what you asked: firing one alert for every reminder that has a time. So the fix is less about finding a magically quieter app and more about choosing how you want your reminders delivered. The options below run from "change a setting, spend nothing" to "adopt a whole new system," and the right pick depends on how attached you are to Apple Reminders.

What actually reduces reminder overload

Fewer, batched alerts

The core lever is collapsing many individual notifications into one moment, whether that is a morning summary or a single daily digest.

Urgent items still get through

Any solution that hides genuinely time-critical reminders trades one problem for a worse one. The good options keep urgent items breaking through.

Keeping your existing reminders

Solutions that build on Apple Reminders cost nothing to adopt. Solutions that replace it mean migrating your lists and history.

Cost model

Free settings, a one-time purchase, and a monthly subscription are very different long-term commitments for the same everyday task.

The honest breakdown

Apple's built-in settings (Today Notification + Scheduled Summary)

Best for: trying the free fix before installing anything

Before any app, Apple gives you two levers. The Today Notification (Settings, Apps, Reminders) bundles your all-day reminders due today into one alert at a time you pick. Scheduled Summary (Settings, Notifications, Scheduled Summary) holds non-urgent notifications and delivers them in batches. Together they meaningfully reduce noise for a lot of people, and they cost nothing.

Where they stop: the Today Notification ignores timed reminders, which are usually the noisy ones. Scheduled Summary is system-wide rather than reminders-specific, and time-sensitive reminders break through it. Neither gives you a single digest of everything due, and neither is an interactive card you can check off.

Remindfully

Best for: one daily digest while keeping Apple Reminders

Remindfully is the featured pick because it targets this exact problem without asking you to abandon Apple Reminders. It reads from Apple Reminders and writes completions back, so Siri, Apple Watch, and the stock app keep working, and it replaces the per-reminder stream with one calm daily digest of everything due today. Reminders set for a specific time can still interrupt, and anything marked High Priority always breaks through. The one-time $4.99 Pro unlock adds a lock-screen Today card you can check items off from without unlocking, plus per-list rules. Free to download, no subscription.

Where it stops: it is iOS only (iPhone and iPad), and it is deliberately a layer on Apple Reminders, so it is the right tool only if you want to stay with Apple Reminders. If you wanted to leave that ecosystem, a full task manager below fits better.

Todoist

Best for: leaving Apple Reminders for a cross-platform system with granular notifications

Todoist is a mature, cross-platform task manager with fine-grained control over which tasks notify you and when, plus a genuine daily and weekly review view. If you are ready to move off Apple Reminders entirely and want the same system on iPhone, Android, web, and desktop, it is an excellent home that can be configured to be far less noisy than default Reminders.

Where it stops: it replaces Apple Reminders rather than calming it, so you migrate your tasks and leave Siri and Apple Watch reminders behind for Todoist's own integrations. Its most useful tiers are a subscription, so this is an ongoing cost, not a one-time fix. Verify current pricing before committing.

Things 3

Best for: an Apple-only power user who wants a calmer, structured system and will pay once per platform

Things 3 is a beautifully designed, Apple-only task manager with a deliberate, low-noise approach built around a daily "Today" view rather than a barrage of alerts. It is a one-time purchase per platform rather than a subscription, which many people prefer.

Where it stops: like Todoist, it is a replacement for Apple Reminders, not a layer on it, so you move your system across and it is purchased separately on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. There is no Android or web version. Confirm current per-platform pricing before buying.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to reduce reminder notifications on iPhone?

The cheapest way is free: turn on the Today Notification in Settings, Apps, Reminders, and set up Scheduled Summary in Settings, Notifications. These reduce noise without any app. They do not give you a single digest of everything due, but for many people they are enough.

Is there an app that gives one daily digest of Apple Reminders?

Yes. Remindfully sits on top of Apple Reminders and replaces the per-reminder alert stream with one calm daily digest of everything due today, while still letting timed and High Priority reminders break through. It is free to download with a one-time $4.99 Pro unlock and no subscription.

Should I switch to Todoist or Things instead of Apple Reminders?

Only if you want to leave the Apple Reminders ecosystem. Todoist and Things are excellent task managers with calmer, more structured notification models, but they replace Apple Reminders rather than layering on it, so you migrate your tasks and give up Siri and Apple Watch reminders in favor of their own integrations. If you want to keep Apple Reminders, a layer like Remindfully fits better.

Do these apps hide urgent reminders?

The good ones do not. Apple's Scheduled Summary lets time-sensitive reminders break through, and Remindfully keeps timed and High Priority reminders interrupting while the routine items stay in the daily digest. Full task managers let you configure which items notify you. The goal is fewer routine interruptions, not silence on the things that matter.

Will I lose my reminders if I use one of these apps?

Not with the built-in settings or with Remindfully, both of which keep your reminders in Apple Reminders. With Todoist or Things you are moving to a new system, so you would migrate your tasks across and your reminders would then live in that app instead.
